## B4. Extension, safety and final decision
TeleconsultBill and CorporatePayer add a 1000-unit bill with 900 covered. Existing Bill, Payer and all previous classes remain unchanged. The earlier four-bill total remains 74700. The original B1 executable is rerun unchanged and keeps all 12 payer checks passing. The B4 ASan run includes a thrown/caught invalid teleconsult charge and exits 0 without diagnostics. This supports the exercised resource paths, not formal proof that no bug exists.

For 15 hospitals adding two bill types and three payers per year, the polymorphic interface is suitable: new classes extend behavior without editing central dispatch. Keep a transactional immutable bill ledger, idempotent claim IDs and explicit retries. At 200000 bills/day, total() is unlikely to be the main cost compared with database/network work; that is engineering judgment, not a throughput measurement. Use exceptions for uncommon failures and error/status results for expected frequent rejections, guided by B3. The current educational in-memory program is not production-ready and does not meet persistence, authentication, financial rounding or regulatory requirements.

Alternative: configuration-driven plugins describe rates/formulas under a versioned schema and validation layer. For m interpreted rules per bill, time is O(m) rather than a fixed virtual-call formula, with O(r) storage for r rule definitions plus per-bill data. A compiled rule dispatcher may reduce this constant but must be measured. New rate-only products can be safer to deploy without recompiling; novel clinical rules still require code, tests, signed releases, rollback and audit. Unvalidated arbitrary expressions/native plugins create security and ABI risks. Configuration does not remove the need for a stable interface.
